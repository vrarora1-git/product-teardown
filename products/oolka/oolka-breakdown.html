What Oolka is

A Bengaluru fintech (Sixdis Technologies, founded 2024) built by Utkrishta Kumar, former Chief Business Officer at Meesho.1,2 The app checks your credit score for free, then its AI agent "Dhruva" finds what is hurting the score and takes action on your behalf: flagging report errors, drafting disputes, reminding and paying EMIs, and recommending loans and cards from partner banks.3,4

The one thing that matters most

The flagship "agentic" action — fixing credit report errors — largely works by drafting and sending emails through the user's own Gmail account. Oolka's own Play Store listing says so.3 Paying users are noticing: the most upvoted recent reviews say the paid product "only drafts emails" and does little else.3 In a business built entirely on trust, that expectation gap is the number one thing to fix. Details on the Hidden truths page.

Why it still deserves attention

Real distribution (6M users in under two years), a real wedge (credit anxiety is universal and underserved in Tier 2/3 India), top-tier backers (Accel, Lightspeed, Z47), and a founder who has scaled consumer products at Meesho. The lending marketplace behind the companion — IDFC First, Yes Bank, AU SFB, InCred, DMI and others — is where the real revenue thesis lives.1,3,4

App & users

The rating says 4.8. The most useful reviews tell a more complicated story.

The headline numbers

4.8★ from 32.2K reviews, 50L+ downloads on Play Store (as of July 2026).3 Independent review analysis shows overall sentiment skews strongly positive, with "cash rewards" as one of the top drivers of positive reviews — users celebrate withdrawing small cashback amounts.8

What the critical reviews cluster around

1. "The score doesn't match CIBIL"

Oolka pulls Experian by default; users compare against CIBIL and conclude the report is "false."3 This is a product communication failure, not a data failure — and it is generating scam accusations.

2. "I paid, and it only drafted emails"

"The part which helps us only drafts the email to the customer service of that overdue account company and nothing else... I paid money to use it and it's completely waste."— Play Store review, 16 May 20263

3. Consent and AI quality complaints

"I never gave auto pay and they added auto pay mandate on the account... its AI keeps switching to Hindi even if I say English, keeps repeating the same things."— Play Store review, 16 Mar 2026, 17 users found helpful3

Single-source on the mandate claim, so treat as an allegation — but it is the most upvoted recent review, and the developer reply was boilerplate.

Pattern worth noting: positive reviews cite rewards and cashback; negative reviews cite trust. The reviews propping up the 4.8 and the reviews other users vote "helpful" are describing two different products.

Language reality check

Oolka says it is designed to support nearly 100 regional languages for the next-billion market.4 Review evidence shows the AI failing to hold even an English/Hindi preference.3 Worth stress-testing before the planned voice agent launches.

Rules & risk (India)

Oolka sits at the intersection of four regulatory surfaces. None is fatal today; all get heavier as it scales.

1. Credit repair is a regulatory vacuum — for now

Under CICRA 2005, disputing a credit report error directly with a bureau is free. Oolka's paid tier sells convenience and follow-through on a right consumers already have at no cost. There is no "credit repair" licence category in India; if RBI or consumer protection authorities ever formalise one (as the US did with CROA), paid repair products get rewritten overnight. Inference

2. Digital lending guidelines

Distributing loans at 14–36% APR from partner NBFCs makes Oolka a Lending Service Provider under RBI's Digital Lending Guidelines: disclosure, grievance and data rules apply, and its bank partners carry the compliance accountability. Review allegations of auto-pay mandates added without consent3 — if a pattern — are exactly what an RBI review of a partner bank would surface.

3. Data: DPDP Act + Gmail scope

The app collects location, personal and financial data and may share several types with third parties.3 It also requests Gmail send access to send disputes on the user's behalf3 — a Google "restricted scope" requiring annual third-party security re-verification. One failed audit or a Google policy shift disables the flagship feature. Oolka claims PII is anonymised before LLM processing.4

4. Payments

EMI payments run over BBPS via an NPCI-authorised BBPOU partner3 — the cleanest part of the stack, and a genuine moat for repayment data.

Hidden truths

Four findings that don't show up in funding coverage or the top review themes. Each is scored on business impact and evidence confidence.

Impact: HighConfidence: HighVerified · 3 sources

1. The "AI agent" runs on the user's own Gmail

The centrepiece agentic action — dispute resolution — works by drafting emails and sending them through the user's Gmail account.3,4 There is no evidence of direct bureau API integration for disputes. Consequences: paying users feel cheated when they see behind the curtain ("it only drafts emails"3), and the whole feature depends on Google's restricted-scope policy. Metric at stake: paid retention, refund rate, platform risk.

Impact: HighConfidence: Medium-HighVerified · 2 sources

2. The 4.8★ is propped up by the rewards loop, and it is masking a paying-user trust problem

Positive review sentiment clusters around cashback withdrawals ("I have withdrawn 480 rupees from this app")8, while the reviews other users actually upvote as helpful are recent 1–2 star trust allegations.3 The store rating is measuring the rewards program, not the credit product. Internal dashboards anchored on it will be blind to the real churn signal. Metric at stake: paid conversion, CAC payback, brand risk in a trust category.

Impact: HighConfidence: MediumPartly inferred

3. The paid tier sells a service that is legally free — with no regulatory moat if that changes

Bureau disputes cost nothing under CICRA 2005. Oolka monetises the follow-through, which is legitimate — but it means the paid product's value collapses if bureaus productise their own dispute UX (CIBIL is already consumer-facing) or if a future rule caps paid credit-repair services. Revenue durability depends on moving monetisation to distribution before that happens. Metric at stake: revenue mix durability.

Impact: MediumConfidence: HighVerified · 3 sources

4. The narrative is "AI companion"; the operating business is loan distribution — collections included

Oolka is hiring Collection Executives9, distributes loans at up to 36% APR3, and the founder openly describes the endgame as a cross-sell distribution centre.4 Nothing wrong with the model — but the further the public story drifts from the revenue engine, the more brittle user trust and bank-partner optics become when the gap surfaces. Metric at stake: positioning integrity, partner confidence.

A fifth candidate — the auto-pay mandate allegation — did not clear the two-source bar and is flagged on the App & users page as a single-source allegation.

What to do

Five moves, sized qualitatively. Two are shippable inside a quarter.

Quick win

1. Fix the score-mismatch complaint at the source

State "This is your Experian score" at first render, with a one-line explainer on why it differs from CIBIL, and a toggle where multi-bureau data exists. This is the most common trust complaint and costs a sprint to fix. Estimate If even 10% of critical reviews stem from this confusion, it is the cheapest rating-repair available.

Quick win

2. Sell the paid tier with an expectation contract

Before payment, show exactly what the agent will do: sample dispute, expected timelines, honest odds. Refund anger comes from surprise, not from the product doing too little. Cuts refunds and the "scam" review vocabulary that poisons conversion.

Structural

3. Replace the Gmail crutch with owned dispute rails

Direct bureau integrations and registered grievance channels instead of sending email from the user's inbox. Removes the Google restricted-scope dependency, and makes "agentic" defensible against GoodScore rather than a demo trick.

Structural

4. Audit consent architecture before the voice agent launches

E-mandate flows, autopay disclosures, DPDP consent records. The bank partnerships are the whole distribution thesis; one adverse RBI/NPCI observation at a partner puts all of them on notice. Voice agents multiply consent ambiguity.

StructuralBiggest upside

5. Make the secured card the hero credit-builder product

Oolka already lists secured cards from IDFC First, Yes Bank and AU SFB3 — but as marketplace inventory. A secured card on FD is the one instrument where the score verifiably improves month after month, which is exactly what the paid tier promises and what emails alone cannot deliver. It converts "improve my score" intent into interchange plus FD economics, and gives the agent a real lever to act on.
